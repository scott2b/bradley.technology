Overstory places current events in historical and geopolitical context. It gathers
news coverage into story arcs, each a story that runs for weeks or months, and then
builds around each arc the context the coverage assumes but does not supply: the
history the story continues, the precedents it echoes, the scholarly frameworks that
explain what is happening, and the open questions that would settle it.

## The arcs

An arc is the unit. The front page groups the active ones into currents, such as
great power security realignment, regional wars and crises, tech consolidation and
governance, and democracy under strain, and marks each arc active or dormant as
coverage rises and falls. On an arc page, the frontier says where the story stands
now, the arc so far is its dated chapters, and a pulse chart counts the distinct
outlets covering it each day. People, organizations, and places named in the
coverage link to reference entries.

## The context

Once an arc has run long enough to be judged history, Overstory builds a reading
layer under it.

**The long view** is a lineage: a dated sequence of the events the story continues,
reaching back as far as the record goes, each entry cited to its source. For a 2026
fight over an attorney general nomination, the lineage runs from the Judiciary Act
of 1789 through the Saturday Night Massacre to the present.

**Echoes** name the historical precedents the story recalls, and a deep-context
essay sets the current events against them, citing every claim to the outlet and
date it came from.

**Lenses** are frameworks from political science, international relations, economics,
and related fields, about thirty of them: two-level games, securitization,
weaponized interdependence, democratic backsliding, deterrence and coercive
diplomacy, the Collingridge dilemma, fiscal dominance. Each lens carries its canon,
the roles it asks you to fill and the questions it asks. An arc read through a lens
has those roles filled by the actors in the story and says what the lens expects to
happen next, and a lens page lists every arc it has been applied to, so one
framework can be followed across stories.

**What it is about** lists the open questions in the arc and what would settle each
one. **Who to read** is a short bibliography of scholarly work on the subject, each
entry with a line on why it bears on the story.
