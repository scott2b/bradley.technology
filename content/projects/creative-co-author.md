Creative Co-author was a writing tool for the first draft: getting words onto the
page, not finishing them. It worked like type-ahead pushed much further. As you
wrote, it offered words and phrases along the way, ahead of the cursor and behind
it. It did not write for you; the suggestions were there to keep a draft moving, on
the theory that stalling can shut down the whole writing process, and that an
imperfect suggestion which provokes a better idea has still done its job.

It ran as a Knight Lab Studio project over two quarters of 2018, before large
language models made text generation ordinary; the suggestions came from the
off-the-shelf language models of the day. {{ person(id="germuska") }} and I led it;
the spring students were {{ person(id="bergstrom") }}, {{ person(id="birori") }},
{{ person(id="dwyer") }}, {{ person(id="osnos") }}, and {{ person(id="xie") }}. The
first quarter worked on how looking ahead differs from lurking behind, what
interface lets a writer take or ignore a suggestion without breaking stride, and
whether suggestions could take on a style or a fictional world. The second built on
that: better word choices for a given context, suggestions at the level of a phrase
or a sentence, and an experience that keeps the author writing from their own mind
rather than reacting to the machine.
