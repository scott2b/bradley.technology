A phone broadcasts over WiFi, Bluetooth, and NFC all the time, and anyone listening
can learn a good deal from traffic nobody meant as public. Privacy Mirror was a
Knight Lab Studio project to make that visible: a mirror that reflects back what is
radiating from whoever stands in front of it, so that people take the exposure
seriously once they see their own device in the list.

{{ person(id="wise") }} led it in the winter 2019 quarter, with
{{ person(id="reinhardt") }} and {{ person(id="wong") }} as the students, and I
provided technical assistance to the team. The quarter's questions were which
signals a person gives off, how accurately a signal can be placed, what can be read
from it, and what makes the result feel like a mirror.
