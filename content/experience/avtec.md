AVTEC Industries manufactured engineered food-service systems for schools, hospitals,
correctional facilities, and other large institutional kitchens: ventilation, energy
and utility distribution, and conveyor-based food-production and tray-assembly
equipment.

I did the electrical and controls engineering on custom systems, working alongside
the mechanical designers, and the job ran through manufacturing engineering, CAD,
product compliance and design automation. Every project was configured to a
particular facility, equipment lineup, and operation, then released as
project-specific drawings and documentation for manufacturing.

## Ventilation and fire suppression

Custom kitchen ventilation systems, in several configurations of exhaust and
supply-air hood. Each one had to coordinate the hood, the cooking equipment under it,
the electrical controls, the ventilation equipment, and the fire protection.

I designed the Ansul fire-suppression systems, including nozzle placement for the
appliances on the line being protected, and wired their operation into the
electrical controls so that a suppression event shut down the equipment and ran the
ventilation as required.

## Energy distribution

Energy distribution systems and raceways run behind a cooking line of fryers, ovens,
and ranges and give the whole line one engineered utility structure: electrical
power, gas, water, appliance connections, controls, and safety shutdown, in place of
a separate building connection for each appliance.

My part was the power-distribution requirements, breaker and appliance circuit
allocation, connections to the specified equipment, and the drawings and control
circuitry. The electrical configuration followed whatever equipment was specified
for each installation.

## Controls

Hard-wired control systems from the industrial parts of the period: discrete relays,
solid-state timers, switches, contactors, solenoid valves, and interlocks, with no
programmable logic controllers. The circuits handled ventilation and wash
sequencing, equipment operation, utility control, and safety functions, and
coordinated fire-suppression operation with equipment and fuel shutdown.

## Tray Trac and conveyor systems

Tray Trac was a circulating tray-assembly system for large institutional food
service: carriers moved around the system while staff assembled meals at
workstations. One installation could combine heated food wells, refrigerated cold
tops and pans, electrical distribution, plumbing, refrigeration, and the conveyor
drive and controls.

## Design automation

AVTEC drew its project and manufacturing drawings in AutoCAD. I wrote AutoLISP
automation for the ventilation hoods and energy-distribution raceways, encoding the
product geometry and engineering rules so that a designer could enter a project's
specifications and have the system generate much of the drawing, in place of
drafting each custom configuration by hand. I also wrote the department's
engineering procedure manuals, which standardized the practices the automation
depended on.

## Manufacturing and compliance

The designs had to be fabricated, assembled, wired, tested, shipped, and installed, so
the role carried through to manufacturing, including UL product compliance: keeping
custom configurations within what AVTEC's listed products allowed.
